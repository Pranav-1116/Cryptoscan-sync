# Market entry plan: Dubai/UAE, Saudi Arabia, and India

**Status:** proposed market-development plan
**Owner:** founder / commercial lead
**Review cadence:** every 30 days during the first 90 days

## 1. Market thesis

CryptoScan should not sell "a scanner" to an undefined region. It should sell a
bounded decision outcome to an organization that already feels one of these
pressures:

- it cannot answer where cryptography, certificates, crypto libraries, or
  vulnerable dependencies are used;
- a migration, audit, customer questionnaire, or regulatory review needs evidence;
- application and infrastructure teams cannot agree which findings are real;
- the organization needs a repeatable inventory before post-quantum or certificate
  migration planning; or
- the security team has tools but not an explainable evidence chain that owners can
  act on.

The first commercial offer is a **six-week supervised pilot** with one primary
objective, not a promise to scan an entire country or enterprise. The pilot should
produce a baseline, coverage statement, validated findings, one remediation/recheck
cycle, and an exit decision.

## 2. Market order

The recommended order is:

1. **India first for learning and referenceable delivery:** the founder is near
   Coimbatore, technical access is easier, travel and support cost are lower, and
   a regional technology or manufacturing customer can give fast feedback.
2. **Dubai/UAE second for high-value financial-services introductions:** Dubai has
   a concentrated financial and fintech ecosystem and a strong need for secure,
   explainable technology adoption. Enter through a local sponsor, accelerator,
   systems integrator, or regulated customer—not by implying government
   endorsement.
3. **Saudi Arabia third through a local relationship and a compliance-aware
   partner:** Saudi opportunities can be substantial, but procurement, data
   residency, Arabic/localization, local delivery, and national-cybersecurity
   expectations require preparation. Do not treat a regulator or accelerator as a
   customer reference unless it explicitly becomes one.

This ordering is a hypothesis. Replace it when measured data shows a different
conversion rate, sales cycle, or deployment constraint.

## 3. Who buys and who helps

### Buyer roles

The first meeting should normally include one business sponsor and one technical
owner. Relevant roles are:

- CISO, deputy CISO, or head of cybersecurity;
- head of application security / product security;
- head of infrastructure, cloud security, or platform engineering;
- cryptography, PKI, certificate, HSM, or IAM owner;
- security architecture or cyber-risk leader;
- enterprise architecture / technology-risk leader;
- CIO office transformation or innovation lead; and
- for a smaller company, CTO or founder with security responsibility.

### Influencers and routes

- **MSSPs and security consultancies:** can introduce several customers and help
  with local delivery, but require a clear partner margin and no unapproved
  resale claim.
- **Fintech accelerators and innovation hubs:** useful for customer discovery and
  proof-of-concept introductions, not evidence that the product is approved.
- **Systems integrators and cloud partners:** useful when the customer needs
  deployment, network, IAM, or data-residency support.
- **Incubators and technology parks:** useful for local credibility, hiring, and
  introductions in India.
- **Regulators and government cyber agencies:** contact for published programmes,
  guidance, responsible disclosure, or ecosystem routes; do not pitch them as if
  they are a commercial buyer unless their procurement path says otherwise.

## 4. Dubai and UAE

### Best first segments

1. DIFC/financial-services technology firms and fintechs;
2. UAE banks, payment companies, exchanges, and insurance technology teams;
3. managed security providers serving financial and government customers;
4. large UAE enterprises with certificates, HSMs, Java/.NET estates, or many
   application repositories; and
5. technology vendors that need an explainable crypto inventory for customer
   assurance.

### Local message

> CryptoScan gives your security and platform teams a bounded, evidence-linked
> inventory of cryptographic use, certificates, libraries, dependencies, and
> binary/firmware evidence. It identifies what was observed, what was not parsed,
> and what remains unknown, so a migration or review can start with facts rather
> than a spreadsheet assembled from memory.

Avoid saying "DIFC approved," "DESC certified," "SAMA compliant," or "regulator
accepted." Say "we can map the pilot evidence to the controls and questions you
select; the customer remains responsible for its regulatory interpretation."

### Public ecosystem routes to verify before use

- **DIFC Innovation Hub / FinTech Accelerator:** use the official DIFC Innovation
  Hub and DIFC newsroom pages to check the current accelerator cohort and
  application route: [`difc.ae/about`](https://difc.ae/about) and
  [`DIFC FinTech Accelerator announcement`](https://difc.ae/newsroom/news/difc-innovation-hub-launches-9th-edition-fintech-accelerator-programme).
  Historical programme pages mention customer proof-of-concept access, but that
  history is not a current guarantee.
- **Dubai Electronic Security Center (DESC):** use the official DESC website and
  official public enquiry route for cyber-sector guidance or partner routing:
  [`desc.gov.ae`](https://www.desc.gov.ae/). The official site should be checked
  for current contact details; do not rely on directory sites or an old personal
  contact.
- **Dubai AI Campus / DIFC contact route:** the published contact page is
  [`dubaiaicampus.com/get-in-touch`](https://dubaiaicampus.com/get-in-touch).
  Use it to request the correct innovation or financial-services introduction,
  not to send a generic mass pitch.
- **Local banks and fintechs:** approach through their vendor-registration,
  innovation, procurement, or security-architecture channels. Start with one
  named business unit and one read-only source, not a country-wide request.

### Dubai first-contact sequence

1. Ask a mutual connection, accelerator mentor, cloud partner, or audit/security
   advisor for an introduction to the security architecture or platform owner.
2. Send a one-page use case tailored to certificates, crypto libraries, HSM/PKI,
   application repositories, or migration planning.
3. Offer a 30-minute discovery session with no customer secrets required.
4. Ask for a 60-minute technical qualification call with the security owner,
   platform owner, and procurement/security-risk representative.
5. Propose a paid or formally approved pilot with a sanitized sample first.
6. Convert only after the customer has reviewed findings and the data/retention
   boundary is accepted.

## 5. Saudi Arabia

### Best first segments

1. Saudi banks, insurers, payment firms, and fintechs;
2. Saudi telecom and large-enterprise technology teams;
3. national or regional MSSPs and GRC consultancies that already hold customer
   trust;
4. cloud and systems-integration partners that can support local deployment; and
5. product companies that need software, crypto, and firmware inventory evidence.

### Local message

> CryptoScan helps the customer produce an explainable cryptographic and software
> inventory that a security owner can validate. It can be deployed as a supervised
> evaluation with read-only sources, a declared data boundary, and explicit
> evidence limitations. It is a tool for the customer's security programme, not a
> substitute for NCA, SAMA, internal audit, or legal advice.

Saudi buyers may expect local hosting or a clear answer about where data and
support access reside. Prepare both a dedicated in-country/customer-controlled
option and a documented answer for the default deployment. Do not promise local
residency, certification, Arabic support, or regulatory acceptance before those
items are actually available.

### Public routes to verify before use

- **National Cybersecurity Authority (NCA) Cybersecurity Accelerator:** the
  official programme page is [`nca.gov.sa/en/innovation-and-investment/cybersecurity-accelerator`](https://nca.gov.sa/en/innovation-and-investment/cybersecurity-accelerator/).
  It is an ecosystem route for cybersecurity startups; it is not customer
  approval. Use the official programme enquiry route and confirm eligibility,
  local-startup requirements, and application dates.
- **NCA general public route:** the Saudi government directory entry is
  [`my.gov.sa NCA`](https://my.gov.sa/en/agencies/17777). Use current public
  contact details from the official page and do not use a staff member's private
  contact information.
- **SAMA Regulatory Sandbox:** use this only if the proposed product/service
  itself is a regulated financial innovation requiring sandbox treatment. The
  official sandbox material identifies `sandbox@sama.gov.sa` as an application
  route in the published framework; verify the current route before sending
  anything: [`SAMA sandbox framework`](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/entiresection/1368).
  A cryptographic-inventory pilot inside a bank is not automatically a SAMA
  sandbox application.
- **SAMA Cyber Security Framework:** use the official rulebook as a customer
  conversation reference, not as a claim that CryptoScan makes the customer
  compliant: [`SAMA Cyber Security Framework`](https://rulebook.sama.gov.sa/en/cyber-security-framework-2).

### Saudi first-contact sequence

1. Use a Saudi partner, accelerator, existing customer advisor, or local
   technology network for the introduction.
2. Prepare a two-page English/Arabic summary of scope, data residency, support
   model, roles, and pilot outputs.
3. Ask for the customer's security architecture, cyber-risk, and procurement
   owners—not only an innovation team.
4. Offer a design workshop covering SAMA/NCA-relevant questions selected by the
   customer; do not label the output as regulatory approval.
5. Start with a local or customer-controlled deployment if residency or support
   access is a gating requirement.
6. Agree on a local incident/support escalation route before connecting real
   data.

## 6. India

### Best first segments

1. Coimbatore/Tamil Nadu technology, manufacturing, SaaS, and industrial
   companies with a small security team;
2. Indian fintechs, NBFCs, payment companies, and banks with certificate,
   application, and crypto-migration pressure;
3. software product companies and OEMs with binary/firmware supply-chain needs;
4. MSSPs, audit firms, and cloud partners serving regulated Indian customers; and
5. incubators and technology parks that can provide design partners and hiring
   referrals.

### Local message

> CryptoScan is an explainable inventory and evidence workflow for security and
> platform teams. A customer can begin with repositories, certificates, SBOMs,
> or a small binary sample, validate the findings with its own experts, and then
> decide whether to expand. The pilot does not ask for private keys and does not
> claim that an empty result proves the whole estate is clean.

### Public routes to verify before use

- **Startup India:** use the official help and incubator routes at
  [`startupindia.gov.in/contact-us`](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/contact-us.html)
  and the official incubator listings. The published helpline is a programme
  route, not a customer lead list.
- **MeitY Startup Hub / SAMRIDH:** review the official scheme page at
  [`msh.meity.gov.in/schemes/samridh`](https://msh.meity.gov.in/schemes/samridh).
  SAMRIDH is an accelerator-support programme; eligibility and cohort status must
  be checked before applying.
- **STPI:** use [`stpi.in/contact-us`](https://www.stpi.in/contact-us) and the
  relevant regional centre. STPI has public routes for technology/startup
  ecosystem engagement; it is not evidence that a customer will buy CryptoScan.
- **CERT-In:** use the official channels for incident or vulnerability
  disclosure, not cold sales. CERT-In's responsible disclosure material is at
  [`cert-in.org.in/RVDCP.jsp`](https://www.cert-in.org.in/RVDCP.jsp). Do not
  send customer scan output to CERT-In unless the customer authorizes it and the
  submission fits the official process.
- **Coimbatore ecosystem:** begin with local incubators and technology parks such
  as FORGE, PSG STEP, AIC RAISE, Amrita TBI, TNAU ABIS, and university networks.
  Verify each programme's current website and intake route; public directory
  listings are leads, not endorsements.

### India first-contact sequence

1. Start with 20 local or reachable accounts where a founder can obtain a warm
   introduction.
2. Offer a fixed-scope technical workshop using a sanitized repository or a
   deliberately selected binary—not an unrestricted scan.
3. Involve the application owner and platform/security owner in the first pilot
   call.
4. Give the customer a repeatable validation workbook: observed, missed,
   duplicate, unknown, not-evaluated, owner, and next action.
5. Ask for a written problem statement or purchase/evaluation approval before
   accepting real sensitive data.
6. Use the first successful customer only as a reference after written permission.

## 7. Country-neutral qualification checklist

Do not accept a lead as a pilot until the following are answered:

- What business decision will the pilot support?
- Which customer role owns that decision?
- What exact systems or artifacts are available?
- Is the data customer confidential, personal, payment, or regulated?
- Can the customer provide read-only access or a sanitized sample?
- Where may the product, database, logs, backups, and support sessions run?
- Who can approve scanning and who can stop it?
- What is the success metric and what is the failure condition?
- Is there a budget owner and procurement route?
- Can the customer review findings within the six-week period?
- What happens to data at exit?

## 8. Outreach scoring

Score each account from 0–2:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain | no stated problem | general interest | named audit/migration/inventory decision |
| Access | no route | cold contact | warm sponsor or formal programme |
| Technical fit | no approved source | possible sample | read-only source ready |
| Authority | no buyer identified | influencer only | security/platform sponsor identified |
| Timing | no event | future project | decision in 90 days |
| Trust | no local route | early relationship | partner/reference/approved vendor route |

Prioritize accounts scoring **8 or more**. Do not inflate the score because a
person replied to an email; interest without scope, authority, or access is not a
pilot.
