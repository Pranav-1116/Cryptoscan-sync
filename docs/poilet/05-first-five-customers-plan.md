# First-five-customer acquisition plan

**Status:** proposed 90-day execution plan
**Commercial boundary:** sell a paid, bounded supervised pilot or a written evaluation agreement. Do not call an unpaid experiment a customer, and do not convert a pilot into unattended regulated production by implication.

## 1. What counts as a customer

For this plan, a first customer is an organization that has:

- a named sponsor and source owner;
- accepted a written scope and data boundary;
- completed procurement or a valid evaluation agreement;
- paid for the pilot/evaluation or signed an approved commercial commitment;
- supplied an authorized source; and
- completed a review of findings and limitations.

A friendly demo, accelerator introduction, verbal interest, or sample uploaded by a
founder is a **lead** or **design conversation**, not a customer.

## 2. First-five account portfolio

The goal is five different but related learning opportunities, not five random
logos. Use these as account archetypes; do not publish or imply that any named
organization is a prospect until the relationship is real.

| Customer | Account profile | Primary pain | Sponsor / champion | Safe first scope | Why this order |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coimbatore/Chennai/Bengaluru security-conscious SaaS or product company | fragmented crypto/library/dependency inventory across a few repositories | CTO/CISO or head of product security; DevSecOps champion | one service and CI artifact | fast founder access and short feedback loop |
| 2 | Indian industrial software/OEM/manufacturing technology supplier | firmware/binary and certificate visibility for a product release or customer questionnaire | product security/engineering head; PKI or release owner | 5–20 approved binaries/firmware artifacts, static only | validates binary evidence and limitations without a network scan |
| 3 | Indian fintech, payment, NBFC technology vendor, or financial-services supplier | certificate/algorithm inventory and evidence for risk/migration review | CISO/application security; CIO/risk economic buyer | one application/repository plus approved certificate metadata | tests regulated-sector security review and evidence workflow |
| 4 | Dubai-based fintech, bank technology supplier, or security consultancy | customer assurance, cryptographic inventory, and a repeatable PoC for financial customers | security architecture lead or consultancy practice head; innovation sponsor | one customer-approved sample and customer-controlled deployment discussion | creates UAE reference path without claiming government endorsement |
| 5 | Saudi cybersecurity/fintech ecosystem partner or enterprise technology supplier | local delivery of a bounded inventory/crypto evidence workflow | MSSP/partner CTO or enterprise CISO; procurement/local delivery champion | partner lab/sample first, then one end-customer scope | tests local partner, residency, Arabic/procurement, and channel economics |

A second Indian account may replace any overseas account if it produces a paid,
well-scoped contract faster. Market balance is a learning objective, not a quota.

## 3. Target-account profile

Prioritize organizations with at least five of these signals:

- 50–2,000 engineers or a product with many deployed versions;
- an identified CISO, security engineering, product-security, PKI, or platform
  owner;
- a current certificate, migration, SBOM, customer-assurance, or cyber-risk
  project;
- repositories, build artifacts, binaries, or certificate metadata that can be
  shared read-only;
- an upcoming audit/customer questionnaire/migration decision within 90 days;
- a cloud/DevSecOps team able to run a bounded evaluation;
- a local security partner or procurement route;
- willingness to name a validation set and review findings weekly; and
- an economic buyer who can authorize a paid evaluation.

Deprioritize accounts that:

- ask for a free unrestricted full-estate scan;
- cannot name a source owner or stop authority;
- demand "100% coverage" before defining a source and denominator;
- want a regulator endorsement or certification claim from the product;
- require production deployment before a security review;
- will not accept data/retention terms; or
- have no decision date or budget path.

## 4. Lead sources and referral paths

### India

- founder and engineering networks in Coimbatore, Chennai, Bengaluru, and
  Hyderabad;
- local incubators, technology parks, university startup cells, and FORGE-type
  innovation networks;
- cloud, DevOps, application-security, audit, and MSSP partners;
- Startup India and STPI ecosystem routes for introductions, not guaranteed leads;
- security conferences/meetups where a permission-based conversation is possible;
- existing vendors who already serve a target's engineering or risk team; and
- public company security/vendor-registration forms.

CERT-In's responsible-disclosure channel is for vulnerability reporting, not cold
sales. Use it only for its published purpose.

### Dubai/UAE

- DIFC Innovation Hub and current accelerator/innovation programme routes;
- Dubai AI Campus / DIFC public get-in-touch route;
- DESC public ecosystem/enquiry routes where the question fits the published
  purpose;
- UAE cloud partners, MSSPs, audit firms, and financial technology advisers;
- fintech founders and regulated-technology communities; and
- customer procurement/vendor portals.

### Saudi Arabia

- NCA Cybersecurity Accelerator public programme route, if eligibility/current
  cohort fits;
- Saudi MSSPs, cloud integrators, fintech advisors, and enterprise technology
  suppliers;
- SAMA sandbox only for a genuinely relevant regulated-financial innovation path,
  after checking current eligibility; it is not a shortcut for a cybersecurity
  vendor sale;
- public NCA/government programme routes for ecosystem guidance; and
- warm introductions through a Saudi sponsor who understands procurement and
  data-residency expectations.

Public programme pages and contact details must be rechecked before every campaign.
A programme introduction is not a customer validation or endorsement.

## 5. Channel strategy

Use a three-lane mix:

1. **Warm lane — 50% of effort:** ask an existing trusted contact for a role-based
   introduction. Give them a one-sentence problem statement and permission to
   decline.
2. **Partner lane — 30%:** approach MSSPs, cloud/security consultancies,
   incubators, and technology partners with a co-delivery hypothesis. Qualify the
   partner before promising a referral fee, margin, exclusivity, or territory.
3. **Direct lane — 20%:** send a short, personalized message to a public company
   channel or role-based address. One or two respectful follow-ups are enough.

Never bulk-message a list. Record source, date, role, opt-out, and next action in
the CRM.

## 6. Outreach templates

### Warm introduction request

> Hi [name], I am looking for one security/product/platform leader at a [company
> type] who is dealing with [specific inventory, certificate, crypto migration, or
> evidence problem]. We are offering a six-week supervised pilot using a bounded,
> read-only scope; it does not collect private keys or claim compliance approval.
> Would you be comfortable introducing me to the person who owns that problem? If
> not, no issue—please do not forward this without their consent.

### Direct role-based email

> Subject: A bounded crypto-inventory pilot for [company/team]
>
> Hello [team/role],
>
> [Company] appears to work with [product/software/financial technology context].
> We are speaking with security and platform owners who need evidence of where
> algorithms, certificates, libraries, and dependencies are used before a risk or
> migration decision. CryptoScan can be evaluated against one customer-approved
> repository, certificate set, or binary sample in a supervised six-week pilot.
>
> The output distinguishes observed, unknown, unsupported, refused, and truncated
> states; it does not request private keys, and a static binary result is not
> presented as a live network observation. We do not claim regulator approval or
> complete estate coverage. Is a 25-minute discovery call with the security or
> platform owner appropriate? If another role owns this, a pointer is welcome; if
> not relevant, we will not continue contacting you.

### Partner message

> We are looking for one or two design partners for a bounded cryptographic and
> software-inventory pilot. Your team could bring the customer relationship and
> deployment context; CryptoScan would provide the supervised evidence workflow,
> technical runbook, and report. We would agree scope, data handling, commercial
> ownership, support, and any referral economics in writing. We will not use your
> logo, claim endorsement, or scan a customer without that customer's written
> authorization. Could we compare your customers' recurring inventory/evidence
> pain with our current pilot boundary?

### Follow-up after a meeting

> Thank you for the discussion. My understanding is [problem], owned by [role],
> with a possible first scope of [source]. The open gates are [authorization/data
> classification/access/procurement]. I have attached only the proposed pilot
> outline. We will not request real data or run a scan until [sponsor], [source
> owner], and [authorization] are confirmed. If this is not a priority in the next
> 90 days, please say so and I will close the loop.

### Arabic localization note

For UAE/Saudi, provide an Arabic translation reviewed by a qualified speaker for
customer-facing material, but keep the technical terms and scope unambiguous. Do
not rely on machine translation for contractual, regulatory, or security claims.

## 7. First meeting agenda: 45 minutes

| Minutes | Topic | Output |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–5 | introductions and role confirmation | sponsor, technical owner, economic buyer gap |
| 5–12 | customer's decision and current process | named pain, deadline, baseline |
| 12–20 | source/data/access constraints | candidate source, classification, residency |
| 20–28 | show a safe, sanitized workflow | evidence chain and explicit limits |
| 28–36 | define smallest useful pilot | scope, success measure, stop condition |
| 36–41 | procurement/security path | NDA/DPA, vendor review, budget owner |
| 41–45 | next actions | named owner/date for charter workshop |

Do not spend 45 minutes on a generic feature tour. The prospect should speak
more than the vendor in discovery.

## 8. Qualification rubric

Score each category 0–2. A pilot proposal normally requires **12/18 or higher**,
with no zero in sponsor, source, authorization, or decision timing.

| Category | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | curiosity only | broad concern | named risk/migration/evidence decision |
| Sponsor | no owner | influencer | CISO/security/platform sponsor |
| Source | no source | possible sample | source and owner identified |
| Authorization | unwilling/unclear | verbal intent | written process and stop authority |
| Data fit | prohibited/highly sensitive without plan | classification pending | bounded, acceptable, minimized |
| Technical fit | no access route | manual sample possible | approved read-only route |
| Timing | no deadline | 6–12 months | decision in 90 days |
| Economics | no budget path | exploratory | budget owner/procurement path |
| Expansion | random feature requests | possible adjacent source | clear next scope if validated |

### Disqualification or nurture conditions

- "Scan everything first and we will discuss permission later." Stop.
- "Show us another customer's data." Stop.
- "Say this makes us compliant." Reframe and continue only if the customer accepts
  evidence mapping without an approval claim.
- No source owner, authorization path, or review capacity. Nurture, do not pilot.
- Free full-estate demand. Offer a paid bounded assessment or decline.

## 9. Pilot offer structure

Use a short proposal with these sections:

1. customer decision and current baseline;
2. one primary objective and optional secondary objective;
3. exact sources and exclusions;
4. deployment/data-flow and support access;
5. read-only credentials and limits;
6. six-week plan and named responsibilities;
7. success criteria and stop conditions;
8. deliverables: validated evidence, report/export, re-scan, and exit record;
9. known product limitations and what the pilot does not prove;
10. price, payment milestone, and any credit toward a later subscription—if agreed;
11. legal/security prerequisites; and
12. expiry date and next decision.

A paid pilot may be credited toward a later subscription only as a proposed
commercial term in the executed order, not as an assumption. Do not publish a
price list until packaging and support cost are measured.

## 10. Pilot-to-paid conversion

### Conversion signals

- sponsor uses the evidence in a live security/risk/migration decision;
- customer completes the validation sample and asks for an adjacent source;
- at least one finding or inventory result has an assigned owner and action;
- the customer accepts the unknown/unsupported limitations instead of asking for a
  green dashboard;
- platform/security owner can operate the workflow with documented support; and
- procurement has a named next step and budget owner.

### Conversion meeting

Show, in order:

1. original decision and baseline;
2. declared scope and coverage denominator;
3. evidence-backed finding/inventory examples;
4. validation outcomes, including misses and unknowns;
5. safe remediation/re-scan;
6. operational effort and support needs;
7. proposed next scope with a new authorization; and
8. commercial options and decision date.

### Paid continuation choices

- one additional bounded source for a fixed evaluation;
- a time-bound subscription with a documented support scope; or
- a customer-controlled deployment/expansion project with separate readiness and
  security terms.

If the customer cannot identify a decision or budget after the final report, close
the pilot cleanly and ask for permission to re-engage later. Do not leave data or
access open in hope of conversion.

## 11. Weekly activity targets for 90 days

These are operating targets, not guaranteed outcomes.

| Activity | Weeks 1–4: learn | Weeks 5–8: qualify | Weeks 9–12: convert |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| new target accounts researched | 15/week | 10/week | 8/week |
| warm introduction requests | 8/week | 8/week | 5/week |
| personalized direct messages | 6/week | 6/week | 4/week |
| partner conversations | 3/week | 3/week | 2/week |
| discovery meetings | 4/week | 5/week | 5/week |
| technical qualification workshops | 1/week | 2/week | 2/week |
| pilot proposals | 0–1/week | 2/week | 2/week |
| active pilot reviews | 0–1/week | 1–2/week | 2–3/week |
| written next-step/close decisions | 2/week | 3/week | 4/week |

If response quality drops, reduce volume. A respectful, relevant contact is more
valuable than a larger list.

## 12. Funnel model for five customers

A planning model—not a forecast—is:

```text
100 researched target accounts
  -> 40 warm/permissioned conversations
  -> 25 discovery meetings
  -> 12 technically qualified opportunities
  -> 8 written pilot proposals
  -> 5 signed/paid bounded pilots or evaluations
  -> 2–3 paid continuations to establish repeatability
```

Review the actual conversion at the end of each month. If the first five only
produce free pilots or no decision, the learning is about offer/qualification, not
a reason to double outreach volume.

## 13. CRM fields and weekly metrics

Record for every account:

- country, industry, size band, and account archetype;
- public source/referral path and date verified;
- contact role (not private personal data unless voluntarily provided for business
  contact and handled lawfully);
- problem statement and decision date;
- sponsor, source owner, economic buyer, procurement owner;
- source type and data classification;
- score and disqualification reason;
- last contact, opt-out, and next action;
- pilot status, scope, value, and open gates;
- proposal value/status and decision date; and
- outcome: won, lost, nurture, or stopped for safety/data reasons.

Track weekly:

- reply rate by channel and market;
- warm-intro-to-meeting rate;
- discovery-to-qualified rate;
- qualified-to-proposal rate;
- proposal-to-signed-pilot rate;
- days from first meeting to authorization;
- pilot completion rate;
- time to first reviewable evidence;
- number of scope changes and safety stops;
- customer validation/false-positive/unknown counts;
- pilot gross delivery effort; and
- conversion to paid continuation.

Never make employee activity (emails sent, calls made) the only success metric.

## 14. 90-day schedule

### Days 1–14: instrument and learn

- select 100 candidate accounts across the five archetypes;
- verify official programme/contact pages and record source dates;
- create the one-page problem/pilot summary and sanitized demo;
- ask for 20 warm introductions;
- conduct at least eight discovery conversations;
- reject accounts without a legitimate authorization path; and
- capture exact customer language for the next message.

### Days 15–30: qualify

- narrow to 20 high-scoring accounts;
- run technical qualification workshops;
- prepare two or three scope-specific charters;
- confirm legal/data/procurement gates;
- offer one India pilot first if overseas gates are slow; and
- do not run a real scan before written authorization.

### Days 31–60: deliver and learn

- start the first one or two pilots;
- keep outreach to the next account cohort;
- hold weekly evidence reviews with the customer owner;
- measure source fidelity, unknowns, validation, and delivery effort;
- resolve product gaps that repeat across accounts; and
- request no public reference yet unless the customer volunteers and approves it.

### Days 61–90: convert and select the next cohort

- complete reports and exit records;
- run conversion meetings with economic buyers;
- sign paid continuation or close/delete cleanly;
- start the next India/UAE/Saudi opportunity only with a partner or approved route;
- decide which archetypes to double down on; and
- update the hiring trigger based on actual delivery workload.

## 15. First-five account review template

For every proposed account, answer in one page:

- Why this account and why now?
- What exact decision is blocked?
- Which role owns it and who pays?
- What one source can be authorized safely?
- What will the product show, and what will it not show?
- What is the customer's baseline and success metric?
- What legal, residency, procurement, and identity gates apply?
- What is the stop condition?
- What is the next paid scope if successful?
- Who owns the next action and by what date?

If the team cannot answer these questions, the account is not ready for a pilot—no
matter how prestigious the logo appears.
